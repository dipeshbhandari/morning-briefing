US equities & consumer policy

Trump reopens the 10% credit-card cap debate

Trump said Saturday he would work with the incoming Democratic Congress on a credit-card interest-rate cap; AP reported the standard proposal at 10%, versus a roughly 22.2% median in an August New York Fed survey.[6] This is renewed political support, not an enacted cap or an immediate change in borrowing costs.[6]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: the renewed bipartisan opening creates policy risk for card issuers; banks argue a cap could restrict credit for weaker borrowers, so consumer savings and credit availability should not be conflated.[6]

Confidence: High · dated evidence

Yardeni cuts the 2026 S&P target to 7,500

In a Saturday-evening note, Yardeni Research lowered its year-end S&P 500 target from 7,900 to 7,500 while arguing that a recession-driven bear market was not its base case.[1] Friday carry-forward, not Saturday trading: the index ended November 20 at 6,910.39; Yardeni put its forward P/E at 16.4.[1][8]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: the new target is a fresh revision after the selloff, but the non-recession thesis remains the research firm’s forecast rather than evidence of a market bottom.[1]

Confidence: High · dated evidence
