US equities & earnings

AI selling pushes US benchmarks into correction

The S&P 500 closed November 20 at 6,910.39, down 3.7%; the Dow finished at 48,147.33, down 1.9%, and the Nasdaq at 22,617.73, down 5.3%, according to AP’s closing report.[6] Reuters confirmed corrections in the S&P and Dow and the Nasdaq’s worst weekly decline since March 2020.[7] Nvidia fell 13.8%, AMD 13.3% and Micron 13.9%; the Philadelphia semiconductor index lost 10.7%.[6] Anthropic fell 9.1% on its second trading day.[6] Ameriprise’s closing snapshot showed Information Technology down 7.1%, contrasting with Consumer Staples’ 0.7% gain.[3]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: NYSE breadth of 3.77 decliners per advancer shows risk beyond megacaps, without establishing an economic recession.[7]

Confidence: High

Intuit falls after guidance reset

Intuit lost 12.8% on Friday after cutting its annual revenue-growth forecast and agreeing to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for about $3.68 billion, Reuters reported.[7] Its prior-evening earnings release showed quarterly revenue up 12% to $4.4 billion and non-GAAP diluted EPS up 56% to $2.91.[4]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: the stock reaction highlights how guidance and portfolio decisions can dominate backward-looking earnings growth.[4][7] The proposed Mailchimp disposal is expected to close in early 2027; it is an agreement, not a completed sale.[4]

Confidence: High

Economy, rates & credit

Cook keeps tightening in focus

Fed Governor Lisa Cook’s November 20 speech estimated October PCE inflation at 3.8%, core PCE at 3.2%, and unemployment at 4.1%.[1] She called growth strong and accelerating and reiterated her dissent in favor of another quarter-point increase at October’s meeting.[1]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: resilient activity and persistent inflation leave a policy constraint even as equities sell off.[1] These inflation figures are Cook’s estimates, not a newly released official PCE report; Reuters identified the coming week’s PCE figures as a policy catalyst.[1][7]

Confidence: High

Technology, AI & hardware

Inkling 2 Mini: local deployment, vendor benchmarks

Thinking Machines announced open-weight Inkling 2 Mini on November 20, with inference and customization available through Tinker.[18] The company describes a 26.4-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model, with 3.4 billion active parameters, whose middle layers run three times per token.[18] It says the roughly 20GB memory footprint enables local operation and reports 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 46.5% on Vals Index v2.2; these are vendor-reported results, not independent validation in this brief.[18]

Why it matters

Apollo and Goldman Sachs announced pricing of approximately $44 billion in senior and subordinated notes to purchase GPUs and related equipment for SpaceX’s terrestrial compute buildout.[19] Their release says the transaction was oversubscribed and enlarged, targets NVIDIA Vera Rubin chips, and expects equipment delivery and lease commencement in the first half of 2027.[19]

Military-AI safeguards and chip limits coexist

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on November 20 that Trump and Xi agreed humans should remain involved in military decisions using AI and that major military actions should not be autonomous, Reuters reported.[21] Separately, Trump told reporters that only older chips should be sold to China and mentioned a comprehensive deal without supplying details.[22] Bessent said licensed H200 sales remained the most advanced chip access available to China.[21]

House Homeland Security chair Andrew Garbarino and House China committee chair John Moolenaar urged Trump to back lower-cost American AI tools on November 20, Reuters reported.[23] They called for access to federal computing resources for US open-weight developers and for government agencies to adopt those companies’ products.[23]
