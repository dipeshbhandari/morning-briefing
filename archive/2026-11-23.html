US equities & earnings

Wall Street rebounds; AI concentration remains the story

The S&P 500 rose 4.7%, Nasdaq Composite 6.5% and Dow 3% Monday; AP says the S&P recovered just over half its previous week’s loss.[7] The Motley Fool’s closing recap put Nvidia up 31.48% and Micron up 19.04%, illustrating the rebound’s concentration in AI-linked shares.[5] AP reports FactSet’s estimate of nearly 33% year-over-year summer-quarter earnings-per-share growth for S&P companies; this is a reporting-season estimate, not a new Monday result.[7]

CrowdStrike deal and early earnings complicate the rebound

Hammerstone’s November 23 review reports CrowdStrike’s proposed all-stock Zscaler acquisition at about $43.9 billion, exchanging 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share.[20] It reports early Q3 EPS of $0.34 against $0.31 consensus and revenue of $1.58 billion, up 28%, versus a $1.53 billion estimate; the review does not specify the EPS accounting basis.[20] It also reports Q4 revenue guidance of $1.638–$1.644 billion and a share-price decline despite the beat-and-raise.[20]

Why it matters

Europe, Asia & emerging markets

Europe rallies, but guidance still separates winners

The STOXX 600 closed 1.9% higher at 637.84, its strongest day since early May; technology gained 4.7%, Reuters reports.[1] AT&S rose 13%, BE Semiconductor 9.2% and Aixtron 8.9%; Europe’s technology index nevertheless remained nearly 13% lower for the quarter.[1] Drax gained more than 5% after forecasting annual core profit above consensus, while Julius Baer fell 1.7% despite a 17% rise in ten-month adjusted pretax profit.[1]

Technology, AI, chips & hardware

NVIDIA confirms Huang’s return; investigation continues

NVIDIA’s November 23 filing says its board reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director on November 22, effective immediately; the public release is dated November 23.[21] The board says it received US-government input and considers his reinstatement no national-security risk, while its independent committee investigation continues.[21] The release also describes a national-security/export-control committee and a search for a chief compliance officer.[21]

Amazon buys optimization technology, subject to approvals

Amazon agreed to purchase the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack and bring Decart’s three co-founders, researchers and engineers into Amazon.[9] The company says the teams have worked together since 2025, including on Trainium; the transaction remains subject to customary conditions and regulatory clearance.[9]

Why it matters

Confidence: High · agreement; projected benefits are vendor claims

  • [9] Amazon · Decart agreement

OpenAI and Cisco release temporary Linux runtime shields

OpenAI and Cisco introduced Shirudo, an open-source runtime using eBPF to observe running Linux systems and apply targeted protections while permanent patches are prepared.[10] Operators can monitor before enforcement; the design includes signed shields and TPM-backed records of protections being loaded or removed.[10] OpenAI reports a controlled test in which a Codex-generated shield blocked Java4Shell against running Elasticsearch without a restart.[10]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: this is a specific vendor-reported demonstration and an interim-defense design, not proof of universal exploit prevention or a substitute for patching.[10]

Confidence: High · documented release; effectiveness not independently replicated

  • [10] OpenAI · Shirudo

DeepMind pairs agent search with formal quantum proofs

DeepMind reports Gemini 4 agent teams improving quantum algorithms, checking results with Lean and sharing verified ideas at virtual conferences every four hours.[11] For an inertial-confinement-fusion calculation, it reports an 11.8-fold improvement in a qubit-and-operation cost metric and required error-corrected qubits falling from 5,650 to 4,338.[11] The underlying paper was first submitted November 6: November 23 is the publisher update, not the preprint debut.[13]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: formally verified algorithm improvements are not a deployed quantum-hardware speedup; DeepMind says the scientific simulations still exceed likely early error-corrected machines’ resources.[11]

Confidence: High · research report; no independent replication reviewed

  • [11] DeepMind · quantum algorithms
  • [13] arXiv · quantum submission

MIT explores local small models for analog-chip design

MIT describes SLIC: a small model trained from larger-model demonstrations, then improved with multi-turn reinforcement learning and simulation-based circuit feedback.[12] MIT reports near-GPT-6-Astra performance on its tested design task using fewer computational resources, with tests on unseen structures and commercial processes.[12] The preprint was first submitted November 10; this is a November 23 university report rather than a new-paper release.[14]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: local execution could help protect proprietary design data, but the reported benchmark does not establish generalized production productivity or a new shipping chip.[12]

Confidence: High · university report; benchmark claims attributed

  • [12] MIT · analog-chip design
  • [14] arXiv · SLIC submission
