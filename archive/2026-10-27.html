Durable-goods orders rise; transportation does much of the work

Census reports September durable-goods orders up 1.8% to $345.4 billion after August’s 0.1% increase.[20] Excluding transportation, orders rose 0.5%; transportation itself increased 4.6%, or $5.2 billion.[20] These are September activity data released October 27, not October production measurements.[20]

Why it matters

Housing sales ease; sampling uncertainty deserves attention

New single-family home sales ran at an annualized 676,000 in September, down 0.6% from August, with 8.4 months of supply.[21] Census gives a ±13.8% interval around the monthly sales change, making the tiny reported decline unsuitable for a confident directional conclusion.[21] Median price was $400,400.[21]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: the Census measure is an annualized pace, not the number of homes sold that month.[21] Inventory and financing affordability matter alongside transactions, while one month’s median can move with the mix of homes sold. Avoid treating a statistically uncertain change as a decisive housing downturn.
