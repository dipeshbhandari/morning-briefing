US equities & earnings

Dow loses 849 points; semiconductor shares finish off their lows.

US equities fell November 17: the Dow dropped 848.75 points, or 1.64%, to 50,759.42; the S&P 500 lost 1.03% to 7,340.53, its lowest close since late July; and the Nasdaq declined 0.74% to 24,328.38.[23] Nvidia finished 0.5% lower ahead of its scheduled after-hours earnings, while Micron fell 0.9%, AMD 0.2% and Intel 0.5%; Microsoft lost 2%.[23] XLK technology and XLE energy ETF price returns were -0.91% and -0.52%; fund moves are sector proxies.[4][5]

Home Depot sales grow; annual EPS guidance stays modest

Home Depot reported November 17 that fiscal third-quarter sales rose 4.9% to $43.4 billion, with comparable sales increasing 2.2% both companywide and in the United States.[24] Updated fiscal-2026 guidance calls for approximately 4.5% total sales growth, 1.4% comparable-sales growth and 2% adjusted diluted-EPS growth from the prior year’s $14.69.[24]

Why it matters

Europe, Asia & emerging markets

Europe’s selloff deepens while Japan also retreats.

European shares finished Tuesday 1.8% lower, while Japan lost 1.3%, according to Reuters’ closing wrap and Trading Day commentary.[28][30] Deutsche Bank fell 6% as renewed scrutiny of its past client dealings added a bank-specific shock to the broader technology retreat.[30] Its late wrap put emerging-market equities down 1.31% and MSCI’s global gauge down 1.21%, showing that the retreat extended beyond European technology shares.[30]

Guinea’s refining push leaves gold producers holding unsold bullion.

Guinea’s domestic-refining requirement is disrupting gold exports and tying up miners’ working capital, Business Insider Africa reported Tuesday, citing Reuters industry sources.[33] The restrictions were introduced in June; the Nimba Gold Refinery opened November 1 after an October 6 deadline for exporting existing unrefined stocks.[33] Bureaucratic delays and a slow refining start have left some producers holding inventories worth tens of millions of dollars.[33]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Inference: For exposed miners, export permissions and processing throughput can matter as much as bullion prices when assessing near-term cash conversion.[33]

Confidence: Medium; secondary report

Economy, rates & credit

Industrial output contracts despite an auto-led manufacturing increase.

The Fed’s November 17 release showed October industrial production falling 0.4% after September’s 0.7% increase, while remaining 2.1% above its year-earlier level.[25] Manufacturing rose 0.3%, but output excluding motor vehicles and parts slipped 0.1%; vehicle-and-parts production increased 4.4%.[25] Mining declined 0.8% and utilities fell 4.3%, reversing much of utilities’ September increase.[25]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: The headline decline is not synonymous with collapsing factory activity, given positive manufacturing and the pronounced utilities reversal.[25]

Musalem stays hawkish; bond-yield index eases

St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said November 17 that several rate increases would likely be needed before spring 2027, arguing that current policy was not significantly restrictive.[26] He also acknowledged a less-likely no-further-hikes scenario involving easing supply costs, stronger productivity or slowing AI-related demand.[26] CBOE ^TNX closed at 5.103%, a calculated -1.8 basis points from Monday; this is a yield index, not the official Treasury fix.[14] HYG high-yield and LQD investment-grade ETF prices changed -0.13% and -0.06%; these do not measure credit spreads.[15][16]

Minnesota announces Coinme settlement participation and kiosk exit requirements.

Minnesota’s Commerce Department announced November 17 that it joined a $2.2 million settlement with Coinme over weaknesses in anti-money-laundering and financial-crime safeguards, Valley News Live reported.[31] The agreement covers 34 jurisdictions and took effect October 7: Tuesday’s development was Minnesota’s announcement, not a newly signed nationwide settlement.[31] Coinme must stop operating cryptocurrency kiosks in participating jurisdictions by January 1, 2027, hire an independent compliance expert and address identified deficiencies.[31] Separately, Minnesota had already prohibited cryptocurrency kiosks from August 1, so the reported settlement should not be confused with the start of that state ban.[31]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Chips, memory & datacenters

NVIDIA reports $115.8 billion quarter; Rubin shipments move beyond roadmap claims.

NVIDIA’s November 17 earnings release reports fiscal-third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year, including $107.6 billion from Data Center, up 110%.[37] GAAP diluted earnings were $2.70 per share, versus $2.68 non-GAAP; gross margins were 74.8% and 74.9%, respectively.[37] The company says Vera Rubin NVL72 production and shipments began, with racks running at partners including Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud and CoreWeave.[37] Fourth-quarter guidance is $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, includes an extra week, and assumes no China Data Center compute revenue.[37] In CNBC’s Tuesday interview, interim CEO Colette Kress projected 75% fiscal-2028 revenue growth and reiterated that the board would not reinstate Jensen Huang; these are management statements, not realized results.[20]

NVIDIA adds substrate and packaging commitments, with capacity arriving later.

A separate November 17 NVIDIA announcement expands supply-chain commitments across substrates, advanced packaging, wafers and contract manufacturing.[38] Kinsus plans more than 3.5 times its current NVIDIA-dedicated advanced ABF substrate capacity, with production expected in the second half of 2027 and additional capacity targeted for third-quarter 2028.[38] A new SPIL agreement, supported by multibillion-dollar NVIDIA funding, targets increased dedicated advanced-packaging capacity by mid-2029.[38] The release also references nearly $3 billion of Amkor agreements announced in July and October, which are older commitments rather than fresh November 17 spending.[38]

AI models & enterprise competition

Microsoft attacks agent costs with MAI models and production Maia 200.

Microsoft’s November 17 Ignite announcement introduces coding, cybersecurity, image and voice models, alongside licensed weights customers can customize and run in their own Azure environments.[36] MAI Code 2 is available in GitHub Copilot; Code 2 Flash also has a limited-access Microsoft Foundry preview.[36] Microsoft claims Code 2 delivers 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol’s performance at 40% of its price across four coding benchmarks; this is vendor evaluation, not independently verified parity.[36] Microsoft also says its second-generation Maia 200 accelerator is in production running its largest models, claiming 1.7 times more tokens per watt.[36]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Interpretation: This combines cheaper application models, enterprise deployment control and proprietary inference hardware into a competing full-stack offer.[36]

Confidence: Official announcement; vendor claims unvalidated
