Amazon reports $201.9 billion quarterly sales as AWS grows 39%

Amazon reported quarterly sales of $201.9 billion, up 12%, with AWS sales up 39% to $46.0 billion.[10] Operating income rose but net income declined, reflecting prior-year Anthropic investment gains.[10] Trailing free cash flow turned negative as AI-related property and equipment spending increased.[10]

Why it matters

Inference: AWS growth alongside substantial infrastructure spending strengthens the case that AI demand is translating into cloud revenue, while negative free cash flow highlights the financing and utilization risk of that buildout. Intel reports $16.5 billion revenue and prioritizes Xeon supply

Intel reported revenue of $16.5 billion, up 21%, and forecast next-quarter revenue of $15–16 billion.[11] Management said results exceeded guidance, prioritized additional Xeon supply and expected the PC slowdown through the first half of next year.[11]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Economy & central banks

US Q3 advance GDP accelerates to 3.4% annualized

BEA’s October 29 advance estimate, released at 08:30 EDT (07:30 CDT), shows Q3 real GDP up 3.4% annualized versus 2.2% in Q2.[14] Investment, consumption and government spending contributed; exports declined.[14] Private domestic final sales rose 4.9%.[14] Quarterly PCE inflation was 2.1% annualized, or 2.5% excluding food and energy.[14]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Inference: Firm private domestic demand reduces the case for imminent easing, but the quarterly inflation measures should not be confused with September monthly or year-over-year readings.

Confidence: High: primary disclosure.

  • [14] BEA: GDP

September PCE inflation runs at 3.6% headline and 3.1% core year over year

September PCE inflation was 0.5% monthly and 3.6% year over year; core inflation was 0.3% monthly and 3.1% yearly.[15] BEA released these figures October 29 at 08:30 EDT (07:30 CDT).[15] Nominal spending rose 0.5%, but real spending increased less than 0.1%.[15] Real disposable income fell 0.3%; the saving rate was 3.8%.[15]

Context & sources

Why it matters

Inference: Inflation absorbing most of nominal spending growth suggests pressure on purchasing power despite resilient aggregate GDP. This combination complicates a simple growth-versus-inflation policy narrative.

Confidence: High: primary disclosure.

ECB holds deposit rate at 2.50% while flagging energy-shock risks

The ECB held its deposit rate at 2.50%, refinancing rate at 2.65% and marginal lending rate at 2.90%.[16] It described energy-related upside inflation and downside growth risks, stable underlying inflation and no material wage response so far.[16] It remained data-dependent without pre-committing to a rate path.[16] APP and PEPP portfolios continued declining without reinvestment.[16]

Context & sources

Why it matters
