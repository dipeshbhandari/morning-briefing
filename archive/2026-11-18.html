US equities & earnings

Nasdaq rises, but Dow loses 561 points.

The Dow fell 561.25 points, or 1.11%, to 50,198.17 on November 18; the S&P 500 slipped 0.23% to 7,323.63, its fifth consecutive decline and longest losing streak since June.[27] The Nasdaq gained 0.42% to 24,429.88.[27] Nvidia reversed earlier gains and finished 2.9% lower despite beating quarterly profit and revenue estimates.[27] UnitedHealth lost 10%, Chevron almost 4% and Caterpillar more than 2%.[27] XLK technology ETF rose 0.34%, while XLE energy fell 3.27%; these are fund price returns, not final GICS-sector index returns.[9][10]

Europe, Asia & emerging markets

Europe retreats while the emerging-market currency backdrop worsens

European shares fell about 1% to a two-week low on Wednesday, while UK equities lost roughly 1.3%, according to Reuters’ late Trading Day market summary.[28] The same report described South Korean shares as up 2%, Japan as mixed and China as steady, a materially different regional picture from Europe’s decline.[28] In emerging-market currencies, Mexico, Brazil, Chile and South Africa each fell at least 1%, with Brazil’s real approaching six per dollar; these are currency observations, not emerging-equity index returns.[28]

Economy, rates, credit & FX

Fed minutes point toward further tightening.

Minutes released November 18 describe the October 27–28 meeting, not a new policy decision.[24][27] Almost all participants judged further rate increases likely appropriate under their baseline outlooks; some had favored a quarter-point increase immediately.[24] A couple said an increase larger than 25 basis points could merit consideration at the next meeting if inflation data disappointed.[24] The October decision held the federal-funds target at 3.75%–4% by a 10–2 vote.[24]

Why it matters

Interpretation: The hold should not be read as an easing signal, but neither do these conditional discussions guarantee December action.[24] Officials described broad inflation pressures and AI-related demand straining supply, while the next scheduled meeting is December 8–9.[24]

Oil & gold

Oil settles lower after inventory builds; gold also weakens

Brent futures settled November 18 at $91.95 a barrel, down $1.19 or 1.28%, while WTI settled at $83.26, down $2.75 or 3.2%, Reuters reported.[33] The EIA’s week-ending-November-13 summary independently confirms commercial crude inventories increased 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million barrels, with gasoline stocks rising 5.2 million and distillates 1.6 million.[34] Reuters said continuing Strait of Hormuz shipping risks limited losses but did not outweigh expectations of excess supply.[33] Its late market commentary also described gold falling about 3% to a 14-month low around $3,735 an ounce; that is a reported market observation, not a verified exchange settlement.[28]

Chips, CPUs & accelerators

OpenAI reports AMD design gains, with important benchmark limits

In a November 18 research post, OpenAI reported up to 14% power-performance-area improvement over heavily optimized AMD flagship-GPU intellectual-property baselines and single-digit gains across key blocks of an upcoming flagship CPU.[4] It said some GPU changes had entered a design sent for manufacturing, while other changes were retained for future versions.[4] Separately, its open-source RISC-V experiments favored GPT-6.2 Astra, but used simulation checks and estimated throughput rather than final production-chip measurements.[4]

Reuters reports a China H200 shipment pause despite US approval

Reuters reported November 18 that Nvidia had stopped shipping H200 accelerators to China, citing three people familiar with the matter.[36] Two sources said regulatory and political uncertainty had prevented sufficient Chinese purchase commitments; the report distinguished this commercial pause from the earlier US authorization for limited sales.[36] It also described Beijing’s encouragement of domestic alternatives and scrutiny of US accelerators, rather than reporting a newly announced blanket export ban.[36]
